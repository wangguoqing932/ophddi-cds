# Scope Statement

Frontiers requires a scope statement at submission; it is not published.

This manuscript reports the development and evaluation of OphDDI-CDS, a deterministic clinical decision-support system for drug-drug interactions between ophthalmic topical medications and systemic drugs. The work belongs to pharmaceutical medicine and outcomes research: it examines how interaction risk is graded for a route of administration for which the DDInter export used here does not apply an absorption model, and it quantifies how far unadjusted database severity ratings diverge from absorption-aware assessment. Across the 1,933 registry-matched pairs with a graded DDInter severity, 1,502 (77.7%) were graded lower once systemic absorption from the topical route was modelled (1,341/1,757, 76.3%, among assessed pairs), and 1,206/1,502 (80.3%) of downgrades involved low-tier ophthalmic agents. The manuscript also compares the deterministic cascade with three large-language-model baselines under two configurations that differ simultaneously in model, token allowance and sampling strategy, reports a semi-independent external audit including the 62 cases whose reference labels are free of the system's absorption assumption, and describes an exploratory clinician grade review that is reported as a perspective comparison rather than as validation. The intended audience is clinical pharmacologists, pharmacists and ophthalmologists concerned with medication safety in patients receiving chronic systemic therapy alongside topical ophthalmic treatment. The system is positioned as a mechanism-based second reader, not as a replacement for clinical or pharmacy judgment, and the manuscript states explicitly that prospective outcome-linked calibration is required before clinical deployment.
