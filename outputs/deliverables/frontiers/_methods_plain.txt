Evaluation followed a four-phase protocol: rule consistency, development-phase checks, a semi-independent external audit against DDInter, and a two-expert grade review reported as a perspective comparison rather than an independent assessment, since one rater is a member of the corresponding author's research team (Section 2.5). The principal registry-scale analysis was repeated on the 104 topically administered agents (the nine intravitreal, intraocular and surgical agents are reported separately), and three LLM baselines were compared under five temperature conditions.

Route is modelled through two mechanisms rather than a route field: every ophthalmic agent carries one of six systemic-absorption tiers, the proxy for how much of a topical dose reaches the circulation, and the rule layer encodes route-dependent predicates directly, for example a rule requiring the systemic_absorption_high flag before it fires.

The registries are summarised in Table 1. Data sources were DDInter 2.0 (302,516 DDI records), FDA ophthalmic product labels, Stockley's Drug Interactions, PubMed, and the British National Formulary. All 1,307 evidence chunks were filtered to approved release and assigned a source tier: A regulatory label, B meta-analysis or systematic review, C original study, D in vitro, animal or pharmacokinetic first principles, E class-level mechanism inference. The tiers describe provenance, not strength of evidence - a label statement (A) can be less direct than a systematic review (B) for a specific pair, and an E-tier mechanism can be biologically plausible but unverified - and they are not weights in the engine. The distribution is A = 53, B = 191, C = 215, D = 584, E = 264 (Supplementary Text S1; what each tier can and cannot support in Supplementary Table S16).

The production system (full_system) is a three-layer deterministic cascade with zero LLM calls (Figure 1; registry counts in Table 1 and evidence-base counts in Section 2.1).

L1 rule engine. Forty mechanism rules (additive beta-blockade, beta-blocker-calcium-channel-blocker effects, additive alpha-2 CNS depression, CYP3A4 induction or inhibition, QT prolongation with low-absorption fluoroquinolones; full inventory in Table 2) are evaluated as entity-flag predicates with match-all or match-any semantics, the highest-severity hit retained. The 43 policy entries comprise the 40 rules plus 3 patient-factor policies; rules are evaluated independently, and the only cross-layer composition is the final maximum of the three layers.

Rule construction, adjudication and provenance. Candidates were generated from DDInter 2.0 pairs linking an ophthalmic agent to a systemic class, ophthalmic product labels, and published case reports or reviews. Severity was assigned in a fixed order: the DDInter level where the pair is recorded, otherwise the label's warning category, otherwise the consequence expected from the mechanism and the systemic drug's therapeutic index. The inventory was reviewed by the study team and the two clinician authors (consensus adjudication; no Delphi panel, blinded review or independent clinical committee). A three-question audit of all 40 rules - does the source concern the named ophthalmic agent, the pair, and the assigned severity and route? - found 2 with direct ophthalmic pair evidence, 5 with contextual ophthalmic evidence, 23 with class-extrapolated systemic-route evidence and 10 with mechanism-only support; severity is directly supported for 2, inferred for 28 and provisional for 10 (Supplementary Table S9; construction protocol in Supplementary Text S10). Four citations naming an unrelated drug were corrected in this revision (digoxin, warfarin and two low-absorption antiplatelet/anticoagulant rules; Supplementary Text S2). The only post-lock rule-severity change was the dexamethasone CYP3A4 rule, downgraded from medium to low on measured exposure below 1 ng/mL.

L2 class matrix. The matrix declares 21 ophthalmic row keys and 34 systemic column keys (33 with defined cells), drawn from the 40 systemic classes, and defines 104 cells: 83 actionable and 21 explicitly marked "none". Eighty-three cells were defined from at least one source; 21 additional cells in the other column are explicitly marked none (not_established, evidence none, manual review), so the matrix contains 104 cells. Each cell records a level, a mechanism, one-line advice and an evidence type. Four upgrade rules require both a class match and a high-absorption or selectivity flag, and an absorption-scaling step forces low, very-low, minimal and none tiers to low on the matrix branch, so no upgrade can raise a low-tier pair. That step is not empirically validated against outcome data; it is an author-specified decision grounded in the principle that a dose which does not reach the systemic circulation cannot produce a systemic interaction, and its behavioural consequence is quantified in Section 3.4. Cell levels were reviewed with the rule set by the study team and were not independently adjudicated; all 104 cells are listed in Supplementary Table S14.

L3 hard constraint and unassessed pairs. Low or very-low absorption with no rule hit and no matrix signal yields low risk (G19). The hard constraint tests two of these six tiers (low and very-low); minimal and none are mapped to low in the matrix layer. Pairs not caught by G19 that reach neither a rule nor a defined cell return low with assessment_status = unassessed; the grade is retained for continuity but is not evidence of safety (Section 3.4). Unknown medications are not silently classified: the engine returns an explicit unknown-drug path and the service raises an error.

Grade definitions and expected management. The grades are screening categories, not probabilities of harm: high, severe harm plausible at ophthalmic exposure or a narrow-therapeutic-index victim with a substantial exposure change (action: prescriber or pharmacist review, monitoring, consideration of an alternative); medium, a measurable but usually non-severe effect or a severe mechanism at low exposure (review and monitoring); low, no mechanism expected to be clinically significant at ophthalmic exposure (no action beyond usual care); unassessed, no rule and no matrix cell (manual review; not evidence of safety). Table 6 gives the full operational definitions, clinical significance, expected management and an illustrative pair for each category.

Absorption tiers. Each agent carries one of six ordinal exposure bands, defined as the expected systemic exposure after topical administration relative to an oral therapeutic dose: high (at least 10%, or plasma concentrations within the systemic therapeutic range), medium (1-10%), low (below 1% but detectable), very low (below assay quantification), minimal (intravitreal macromolecular agents and intraocular agents with negligible systemic exposure) and none (viscoelastics and tear substitutes). The registry contains 12 high, 20 medium, 59 low, 11 very-low, 7 minimal and 4 none agents. Supplementary Table S5 records the evidence and certainty for all 113 rows: 13 measured (six single agents and seven fixed combinations: five timolol combinations whose tier follows the measured timolol component and two dexamethasone combinations whose tier follows the measured dexamethasone component), 11 label-statement and 89 class-inferred (marked uncertain); for the 13 measured rows the exposure metric is recorded, because fractional bioavailability, plasma concentration and relative exposure are not interchangeable; for the 11 label-statement rows the label claim is recorded without a value. The 13 registry-versus-curation discrepancies reported previously have been resolved rather than deferred: eight curation values were corrected to the registry, dexamethasone and its two fixed combinations were re-tiered from high to medium on peak plasma concentrations below 1 ng/mL (TobraDex label), and dorzolamide and brinzolamide were re-tiered from low to very low because plasma concentrations are below assay quantification (full resolution in Supplementary Table S10). Fixed combinations are decomposed into active components (product tier = highest component tier; rule matching on the union of component mechanisms), the six intravitreal anti-VEGF agents are assigned individually (formats and serum-exposure evidence in Table S5; all minimal), and the nine non-topical agents are reported separately (Section 3.4).

Patient-specific factors. The system is pair-level: age, renal function, hepatic function, QT risk, dose, duration, CYP phenotype and instillation technique are not modifiers of the grade. Three contextual policies (elderly, renal impairment, polypharmacy) are returned as separate advisories triggered by narrow-therapeutic-index systemic flags, not applied to the grade; QT risk is represented only through qt_prolonging flags and the QT rules, and CYP phenotype only through substrate/inhibitor flags. Supplementary Table S15 maps each factor to its representation, its effect on the output and the remaining gap.

Intended workflow. The intended use is a second-reader check at the point of prescribing or medication review (Figure 6). The user enters the ophthalmic agent and the systemic drug; out-of-coverage agents return an explicit error, and for covered pairs the service returns the final grade, decision path, matched rule identifiers, source record and management category: high prompts review before starting, medium prompts review and monitoring, low requires no action beyond usual care, and unassessed pairs are referred for manual review. The workflow is proposed, not evaluated in a clinical setting; no outcome or prescribing data were collected.

The evidence base was embedded with a 2048-dimensional Doubao model and indexed with LightRAG (1,307 documents, 4,956 entities, 6,885 relations); retrieval baselines used BM25 over the same chunks.

L1 author-derived reference set (118 cases). Three subsets: 20 cases annotated by the study team from named literature sources, 58 guideline-sourced cases covering classes where the routes diverge most, and a procedurally frozen 40-case blind subset. Twenty labels (G01–G20, tagged T1_literature_consensus) were annotated from FDA labels, Stockley's, BNF tables and Drugs.com, with supporting statements deposited; five differ from the DDInter severity for the same pair, and the remaining 98 were annotated by the two authors from the same categories of source. The 58 guideline cases were drawn from the evidence that specific rules cite: for 47 the reference label is that rule's severity, and 11 were overridden by hand after review (identifiers in Supplementary Table S1). Agreement on the 47 therefore measures rule fidelity by construction, and the groups are reported separately. The initial labels and blind proposals were drafted without reference to system outputs. After the lock and before the questionnaire, on 31 July 2026, three labels were changed to match the engine's grade: B02 (high→medium), B12 (medium→high) and C17 (low→medium); both versions are reported. The primary analysis uses the labels as frozen, before those corrections; the corrected labels are a sensitivity analysis (Section 3.3), and the 40-case blind subset is reported separately. The subset is procedurally frozen but not development-free: C01–C20 entered the class-matrix loop in v2.1–v2.3, and B01–B20 were also reviewed during development (B02 and B12 were corrected after engine review, B18 and B19 informed matrix design, and other B cases informed rule-gap diagnosis). Neither half is an independent test. B02 and B12 are in the B-only subset, so the 20/20 corrected figure inherits both of those changes and is partly tautological, because both were moved to match the engine's grade; C17 belongs to the C-only subset. DeepSeek-chat was used as an assistive tool for part of the reference-label annotation; all labels were reviewed and verified by the authors, and the model did not have access to system outputs. The seventeen frozen-label disagreements are listed with rationale in Supplementary Table S1; reference-level distributions under the frozen and corrected labels are given in Table 4 and Supplementary Table S8.

DDInter audit set (92 cases). A stratified random sample by absorption tier and DDInter severity. The stated rule was to set a case to low when the agent's tier is low or lower, no rule fires and no matrix upgrade applies. As applied, 30 cases were corrected and 62 kept, with three documented deviations (seven corrected cases are in fact reached by a rule; the two aflibercept and four flurbiprofen pairs were kept; full list in Supplementary Text S8). Re-deriving the labels under the stated rule changes 10 of 92, giving 61 of 92 (0.663) with sensitivity 8 of 29, against 62 of 92 (0.674) and 8 of 25 under the applied labels; both sets are reported in full in Table 5. The audit is therefore semi-independent: the severity component is third-party, but the absorption correction shares the system's assumption. A development set (200 DDInter-derived cases), an external co-mention set (70 cases) and an internal development set (78 cases) served as development gates, not validation; the first two carry unadjusted database labels (Section 3.2), and the internal set overlaps 78 of the 118 L1 cases with identical labels. The registry-scale comparison uses the 1,933 pairs with a graded DDInter severity; the export contains 4,546 registry-matched pairs, of which 2,613 carry Level Unknown and are excluded.

pure_llm makes one LLM call with a domain-general prompt and no retrieved evidence. naive_rag adds BM25 retrieval over the same evidence chunks (BM25Okapi, k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75; top-k = 3 passages, each truncated to 400 characters; the query includes the drug names - full prompt in Supplementary Text S3). rule_context (method id lightrag in the deposited code, retained for continuity) adds a knowledge-graph context block rendered by PreciseKG.full_kg_context(), which reads the registries and rule configuration rather than the vector index: the drug profiles, the matched rule identifiers with their severities, and, for low-absorption pairs with no matched rule, an explicit instruction that the grade must be low. The baseline therefore receives part of the rule layer, so the gap between it and full_system understates the cascade's advantage over a retrieval baseline that does not receive rule hints. Two prompt details limit the baseline comparison: the shared system prompt lists dexamethasone as >50% after the tier was corrected to medium (1-10%), and configuration 2 rule_context uses the pre-correction context block. Configuration 1 rule_context was re-run with corrected exposure bands (high >=10%, medium 1-10%, low <1%) and the absorption gate; pure_llm and naive_rag were not re-run. The LLM baseline comparison therefore mixes a corrected rule_context row with uncorrected rows and is not a clean test of retrieval augmentation; the deterministic cascade and its reference and audit results are unaffected.

LLM baselines used deepseek-chat with a 10-token cap and no chain-of-thought prompting (temperatures 0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 and 1.2; run configuration and file-level fingerprints in Supplementary Text S5). Five conditions × four methods × the 480 cases in four datasets produced 9,600 rows with zero API failures. Configuration 2 used glm-5.3-flash with max_tokens 3000, temperature 0.0 and a majority vote over five repeated samples. It differs from configuration 1 in model, token allowance and sampling simultaneously; comparisons between them therefore describe two configurations, not the effect of output budget or of reasoning.

Metrics were exact three-level agreement, high-risk sensitivity and specificity, high-risk F1, and Cohen's kappa with Landis–Koch interpretation. The positive class is high versus non-high; the reported specificity is therefore non-high specificity, and medium-to-low errors are not captured by it. Method differences were tested with the exact two-sided McNemar test on discordant pairs at condition 0; intervals are Wilson, Clopper–Pearson or bootstrap as appropriate. No power calculation was performed. Full parameter lists and prompts are in Supplementary Text S3 and S5.

Two ophthalmologists reviewed 12 cases under a grade-review protocol (Supplementary Table S3). Both are clinicians of the Department of Ophthalmology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, and one is a member of the corresponding author's research team, so we use "perspective comparison" rather than "independent assessment". The instrument displayed the drug pair, a short scenario, the proposed reference grade and a system grade with a one-line rationale, and the expert recorded their own judgment on the same three-level scale; because both grades were visible, this is a grade review rather than a blinded assessment, and the cases were constructed around the system's outputs, so the exercise cannot serve as independent validation. Ratings were collected on 3 August 2026 and transcribed on 8 August; expert 1's questionnaire is deposited as a scan and both sets as transcribed records. Two clinicians are a purposive panel, not a sample of clinical opinion, and the exercise is reported as descriptive.

The system is a research-grade, mechanism-based second reader. It is not intended to replace clinical or pharmacy judgment, out-of-coverage medications are rejected with an explicit error, and it is not marketed as a medical device. The model does not encode dose, duration, patient susceptibility beyond three patient-factor policies, or local ocular interactions; those remain clinical-judgment inputs. Pairs outside rule and matrix coverage that are not caught by G19 are returned as low with assessment_status = unassessed, not as assessed low-risk pairs.
