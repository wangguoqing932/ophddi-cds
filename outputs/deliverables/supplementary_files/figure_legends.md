# Figure Legends

**Figure 1. Absorption-first cascade for ophthalmic–systemic drug–drug interaction screening.**

Ophthalmic agents (113, each with one of six systemic-absorption tiers) and systemic medications (232, with mechanism flags and classes) pass through three layers: **01**, the L1 rule engine — 40 mechanism rules plus 3 patient-factor policies, matched by entity-flag predicates with MAX-MATCH semantics (11.7% of registry pairs); **02**, the L2 class matrix — 21 ophthalmic row keys × 33 columns with defined cells (34 declared), 104 defined cells (83 graded, 21 marked "none"), with absorption scaling applied inside this layer (25.7%); and **03**, the L3 G19 hard constraint (42.9%, plus a 19.7% explicit default-low path). The final grade is the maximum of the three layers. The right column shows the six-tier distribution across the 113 agents (12 high, 20 medium, 59 low, 11 very low, 7 minimal, 4 none) and the released matrix cell by cell. Layer shares are measured over all 26,216 registry pairs; the production path makes no LLM call and returns matched rule IDs with every grade. Source data: `outputs/figures/source_data/figure1_source.csv`; matrix cells from `configs/class_matrix.yaml`.

**Figure 2. Performance comparison under two configurations (118-case author-derived reference set).**

Grouped bars show high-risk sensitivity, specificity, F1 and Cohen's κ for the deterministic cascade and three LLM baselines. **Panel (a)** is the primary comparison: frozen (pre-errata) labels, `deepseek-chat` with a 10-token output cap, mean over five temperature conditions. **Panel (b)** repeats the baselines in configuration 2 (`glm-5.3-flash`, 3000-token allowance, temperature 0.0, majority vote over five samples) on the corrected labels; configuration 2 changes model, token allowance and sampling together, so the panels describe two configurations rather than the effect of output budget. In (a) baseline sensitivity is 0.593–0.779 and exact agreement 0.419–0.714; in (b) sensitivity falls to 0.214–0.536 while specificity rises to about 1.000, because the baselines adopt a conservative rarely-high-risk policy that inflates exact agreement on a set dominated by low and medium grades. The cascade returns 0.857 sensitivity, 0.978 specificity, κ 0.779 and exact agreement 0.856 under the frozen labels (panel a), and 0.893/0.989/0.818/0.881 under the corrected labels (panel b). Configuration 1 `rule_context` was re-run with corrected exposure bands and absorption gate; `pure_llm` and `naive_rag` were not re-run. Configuration 2 `rule_context` uses the pre-correction prompt.

**Figure 3. Expert grade review and per-case grades (two ophthalmologists, 12 cases).**

**Panel (a)** shows Cohen's κ (unweighted), quadratic-weighted κ on the ordinal scale low < medium < high, and raw agreement for five comparisons: inter-rater (11/12; κ = 0.840, weighted 0.875), each expert against the displayed grade (expert 1: 2/12, κ = −0.176, weighted 0.355; expert 2: 1/12, κ = −0.257, weighted 0.313), and each expert against the proposed reference grade (expert 1: 4/12, κ = 0.020, weighted 0.473; expert 2: 3/12, κ = −0.091, weighted 0.438). **Panel (b)** gives the per-case grades for the reference grade (identical to the deposited engine grade on all 12 cases), the grade displayed on the questionnaire (which differs from the engine for R10 and R11, where a class-level hint of medium was shown instead of the engine's low), and both experts' ratings. Against the reference grade, all eight of expert 1's discordant ratings and eight of expert 2's nine are lower than the reference grade. The exercise is a grade review between non-independent participants and is reported as a perspective comparison, not a validation (Sections 2.5 and 3.8). Source data: `outputs/figures/source_data/figure3_source.csv`.

**Figure 4. Worked demonstration cases.**

**Panel (a)** shows the rule signals for the worked case (topical timolol with oral atenolol): `additive_beta_blockade` (weight 3) and `systemic_absorption_high_risk` (weight 1). **Panel (b)** shows the final grade of the six curated cases: three high (timolol × atenolol, timolol × verapamil, atropine × amitriptyline), two medium (ketorolac × warfarin, dorzolamide × furosemide) and one low (latanoprost × atorvastatin). For the worked case the cascade returns the matched rule IDs, the decision path (`rule`) and a clinical recommendation.

**Figure 5. Per-step latency of the deterministic cascade (timolol × atenolol).**

Latency of the five cascade steps on a single case: L1 rule matching (matched `additive_beta_blockade`, severity high), L2 matrix upgrade check (non-selective beta-blocker with high absorption, upgrade applied), L3 G19 check (not triggered; absorption high), MAX-MATCH decision (final grade high) and LLM arbitration (not used). Total 1.9 ms on a single CPU; this is one worked trace, not a benchmark across pairs.

**Figure 6. Intended workflow and output contract.**

**Panel (a)** shows the production path: ophthalmic + systemic input, registry check (unknown agents return an explicit error), L1 (40 mechanism rules plus 3 patient-factor policies, MAX-MATCH), L2 (21 × 34 declared keys, 33 with defined cells, 104 cells: 83 actionable and 21 none, absorption scaling), L3 G19, and final grade = max(L1, L2, L3) with path, rule IDs and source record; uncovered pairs are low with `assessment_status = unassessed`. **Panel (b)** shows the result card of the web interface for timolol + verapamil: HIGH result, assessment status `assessed`, one triggered rule, path `rule engine` and recommendation. **Panel (c)** shows the rule-details view: rule ID `beta_blocker_ccb_additive`, severity and mechanism class, rationale with citations, recommendation and matched tags. The output contract is grade, path, rule IDs, source and action; the workflow is proposed, and a grade is a screening flag, not a probability of harm.
